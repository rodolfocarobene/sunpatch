See when the sun enters your home

Pick your building on a map, sketch your apartment and its windows, then watch the patches of direct sunlight move across your floor, for any day of the year.

Open the app Try the Milan example

How it works

  1. 1 Find your building

    Search an address and click your building on the OpenStreetMap map. Its neighbours come along, so their shadows count.

  2. 2 Sketch your apartment

    Choose the floor, draw the outline of your flat, and click to add windows and interior walls.

  3. 3 Watch the sun move

    Slide the date and time. The floors above are cut away so you can see the sun patches travel through the rooms.

  4. 4 Walk inside

    Step into first person and look around. Use the keys to move through the day and see when a room gets its light.

What you get

  • Any date, any hour

    Real sun positions for your location, from the winter solstice to midsummer.

  • Real neighbours

    Surrounding buildings and trees come from OpenStreetMap and cast shadows on yours. Where a height is missing it is drawn striped, and you can set it yourself.

  • Your own plan

    Draw the outline, windows and walls to scale. Fix a missing balcony on the building if the map lacks it.

  • Cutaway and walk mode

    See the apartment from above, or stand in it and look at the window you care about.

  • Sun map

    Colour the floor by its hours of direct sun on the chosen day, and click any spot for the times.

  • Solar panels

    Place balcony or roof panels and read their yearly energy, with shading and local weather.

  • Garden

    Click a spot to see which plants would grow there, month by month, or find the best spot for one plant.

  • Export and import

    Download a plan as JSON and load it again later or on another device.

  • No sign-up

    Open the page and start. There is no account, no install and no cost.

What it does and doesn't model

Models

  • Direct sun only: the beam through each window, with shadows from your building and the neighbours.
  • Building heights from OpenStreetMap (height tag, else levels, else a 9 m guess), which you can correct.
  • Trees: mapped ones exactly, and parks and woods with a scatter of trees.
  • Windows with their own width, sill and height.
  • Solar energy and plant ratings from the direct sun plus local weather averages (NASA POWER).

Does not model

  • Diffuse or reflected light, so shaded rooms look dark.
  • Roof shapes: roofs are flat.
  • Terrain and distant hills: the ground is flat for now.
  • Awnings, most balconies and window frames. Draw the ones that matter with a building fix.
  • Leaves: a tree casts the same shadow all year.

Treat it as a good estimate, not a survey. OpenStreetMap heights are sometimes missing or wrong.

Questions people ask

How many hours of sun will my flat get in winter?

Set the date to the winter solstice and switch on the sun map. Each spot of the floor is coloured by its hours of direct sun that day, and you can click it for the times.

Which rooms get direct sun?

Draw your flat with its windows and slide through the day. The patches of sun show which rooms are lit and when. A room with no direct sun can still be bright: the separate Daylight (estimate) layer gives a rough idea.

Do neighbouring buildings shade my windows?

Yes, if they are on the map. The buildings around yours come from OpenStreetMap and cast shadows. Their heights are sometimes missing or wrong, so you can correct them.

Where should I put plants or a balcony solar panel?

Click a spot in the Garden tab to see which plants suit its sun, or place a panel in the Panels tab and read its yearly energy. Both are estimates, not guarantees.

Is it free, and do I need an account?

It is free and open source, with no account and no install. Your plans stay in this browser and are not uploaded anywhere.

Private by design

Everything runs in your browser. Your plans are saved in this browser's local storage and never leave your device. There is no account and no tracking. The app only contacts the free map services it needs to show the map and find buildings.
