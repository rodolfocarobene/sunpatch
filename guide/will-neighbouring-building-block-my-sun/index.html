Will the building across the street block my sun? How to check before you rent or buy

By Rodolfo Carobene, updated 20 November 2026

A flat that looks bright on a July viewing can be in shade all winter. Before you rent or buy, it is worth checking what the buildings around it do to the sun. The geometry is simple.

A rule of thumb for shadows

A building of height H casts a shadow of length H divided by tan(sun height). In Milan at noon on 21 December the sun is about 21° high, so the shadow is about 2.6 times the height. A 20 m building throws a shadow of roughly 52 m. On 21 June at noon the sun is about 68° high and the same building casts only about 8 m.

In winter the sun sits in the south-east, south and south-west, so the buildings that matter are the ones on those sides of you. A tall neighbour to the north does not shade your south windows.

A worked example: one street, different floors

Say a south-facing street is 15 m wide and the building on the other side is 18 m tall. You look out of a window at the middle of the first floor, about 4 m up.

  • First floor: the roof opposite is 14 m higher than you and 15 m away, so it rises to about 43° in the sky. The noon sun in December (21°) is behind it. Even around the equinoxes (about 44° at noon) it only just clears the roof.
  • Fifth floor, about 16 m up: the roof is only 2 m higher, about 8° in the sky. The low winter sun clears it for almost the whole day.

In June the noon sun is at 68° and clears the roof from either floor. The same street, three very different winters. This is why the floor number and the street width matter more than the street's name.

How to check before you decide

  • Look at which side the neighbours are on, and how tall they are. Counting floors on the opposite facade and allowing roughly 3 m for each gives a decent height.
  • Visit at the time of day and, if you can, the season you care about. A winter afternoon tells you more than a summer morning.
  • Test the flat in a model. In Sunpatch the buildings around yours come from OpenStreetMap and cast shadows on your windows. Set the date to 21 December and slide the time.

For what the winter hours look like inside a flat, see how many hours of sun an apartment gets in winter. To choose a side first, read the guide to south, east, west and north facing flats.

What Sunpatch models and what it does not. Neighbouring buildings and trees come from OpenStreetMap. Where a height is missing the building is drawn striped with a 9 m guess, and heights can also be wrong, so compare them with what you see and correct them if needed. Roofs are flat, trees are rough and there is no terrain yet. It shows direct sun only, never a forecast, so clouds are not included. Times follow your browser's time zone.
